茶の湯 chanoyu

Chanoyu means hot water for tea, and that is all it claims to be. Not a performance, not a religious rite: a meeting, arranged so that nothing in it happens by accident and nothing in it can happen twice.

A host invites a few guests. There is a garden to cross, a door too low to pass standing, a fire, a bowl. Every object has one task and a name. What takes hours to do could be done in minutes, and the difference between the two is the whole of it.

  • 露地 rojiThe dewy path. A garden whose only purpose is to take the street out of you before you arrive. Walk it.
  • 茶室 chashitsuThe room itself, classified by where its hearth is cut. Four positions, each normal and reversed: the eight.
  • 道具 doguNot equipment: named individuals. One object at a time, in a field of nothing.
  • 点前 temaeThe procedure. Sixty movements to make one bowl of tea, arranged so that they look like none.
  • 茶事 chajiThe full gathering. Charcoal, a meal, thick tea and thin, and a season that will not come round again.
  • 立礼 ryureiTea at a table, standing. Devised in 1872 so that guests who do not kneel could still be served, and the way this group practises.
  • 一期一会 ichigo ichieOne time, one meeting. Why the same guests are never invited to the same afternoon.
